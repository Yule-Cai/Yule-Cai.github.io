KA-IHO: A Kinematic-Aware Improved Hippo Optimization Algorithm for Collision-Free Mobile Robot Path Planning in Complex Grid Environments

C. Yuan, Y. Cai, H. Que, Y. Pei, X. Zhang, J. Xie, Q. Zhang, L. Mu, F. Qiao · Sensors · Co-first author

Path planningOptimization
Read the paper ↗All publications
The research question

Can a swarm optimizer produce short, collision-free paths that a differential-drive robot can actually follow?

KA-IHO combines feasible-solution initialization, a hierarchical elite–scout search, stagnation recovery and late-stage path ironing. Tests span five grid maps and hardware tracking.

Approach

  1. Feasible initialization
    An elite safety pool improves the odds of finding usable candidate paths in dense obstacle fields.
  2. Explore without stagnating
    Elite–scout updates, population restarts and radial micro-search help escape difficult local configurations.
  3. Smooth for execution
    A late-stage Laplacian line-of-sight operator removes redundant bends and checks collision safety.

Main findings

  • Strong benchmark feasibility
    Full success is achieved on four of five reported maps; the hardest constrained configuration remains more difficult.
  • Hardware verification
    Differential-drive trials report trajectory-tracking errors within approximately ±4 cm.
  • Smoothness with constraints
    The objective and ironing stage favor feasible trajectories over merely short geometric curves.

Scope and limitations

The 3,000-candidate initialization adds considerable computation. Fixed waypoint count and static-map testing limit straightforward use in larger, rapidly changing scenes.

Paper abstract

A motion-planning optimization study combining heuristic search with kinematic awareness in complex grid environments.
