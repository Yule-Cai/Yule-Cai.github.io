Beyond correlation: a survey on causal inference for dynamics-aware perception and introspective decision-making in robotics

Y. Cai, X. Zhang, S. Liu, J. Lou, C. Yuan · Nonlinear Dynamics · First author

Causal inferenceRobot perceptionIntrospection
Read the paper ↗All publications
The research question

How can a robot distinguish the causes of disturbances rather than merely fitting correlations in sensor and control traces?

This survey organizes research on causal representation learning, interventional discovery, counterfactual reasoning and causal-aware feedback control into a unified perspective for embodied systems.

Approach

  1. Causal representation
    Recover invariant mechanisms from high-dimensional streams instead of relying only on correlated features.
  2. Active discovery
    Study interventions and causal hypothesis testing under limited observation.
  3. Counterfactual recovery
    Connect abduction, hypothetical action and predicted consequences to recovery decisions.
  4. Causal-aware control
    Consider robust closed-loop control and introspection in embodied policies.

Main findings

  • A taxonomy, not a new benchmark
    The contribution is a structured synthesis of prior methods, their assumptions, and where they may fail.
  • Physical confounding
    Shifts in dynamics can invalidate the shortcut relationships exploited by purely correlational policies.
  • From theory to robots
    The synthesis links causal modeling to quadrupedal motion and dexterous manipulation case studies.

Scope and limitations

As a review, its taxonomy and research directions are interpretive synthesis; they should not be read as a new empirically validated causal robot controller.

Paper abstract

A survey examining how causal reasoning can support dynamics-aware robotic perception and introspective decision-making beyond statistical correlation.
