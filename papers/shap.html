Application of SHAP-Based Explainable Machine Learning in Remaining Useful Life Prediction for Aircraft Engine Systems

C. Yuan, Y. Cai, Z. Zhang · International Journal of Machine Learning and Cybernetics · Co-first author

Explainable AIPredictive maintenance
Read the paper ↗All publications
The research question

Can an aircraft-engine remaining-useful-life (RUL) estimate be accurate enough for maintenance planning while also revealing which sensor signals drive it?

Using NASA C-MAPSS turbofan degradation data, this study benchmarks eight machine-learning algorithms and then explains the strongest model with SHAP. The work emphasizes both prediction error and the interpretation of individual features.

Reported results

36.68RMSE · LightGBM · RUL prediction error
26.53MAE · LightGBM · Across the evaluation set
0.713R² · LightGBM · Best of eight models compared

Numbers refer to the specific evaluation in the linked publication; they are not universal performance claims.

Figure from the paper

Approach

  1. Prepare the sensor data
    Clean and transform operational cycles and sensor measurements from the C-MAPSS engine runs.
  2. Compare predictive models
    Evaluate eight algorithms on RUL regression; LightGBM offers the strongest reported overall metrics.
  3. Explain the fitted predictor
    Analyze global importance, SHAP contributions and feature interactions rather than presenting a prediction alone.

Main findings

  • Key predictive signals
    Operational cycles, sensor 11 and sensor 15 have the largest modeled contributions to the RUL estimates.
  • Nonlinear behavior
    SHAP dependence analyses reveal nonlinear effects and interactions, including changes around certain normalized sensor values.
  • A usable interpretation layer
    The results identify which measurements deserve closer attention in a predictive-maintenance dashboard.

Scope and limitations

These explanations describe associations learned by LightGBM; SHAP does not establish physical causation. Evaluation uses benchmark engine data rather than a live maintenance deployment.

Paper abstract

An explainable machine-learning approach for estimating remaining useful life in aircraft engine systems.
