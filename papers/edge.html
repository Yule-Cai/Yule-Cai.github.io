Toward edge-intelligent consumer robots: a decoupled RL–LLM architecture for robust navigation under partial observability

Y. Cai, W. Tan, Y. Wang, X. Du, T. Sun, Q. Ai, S. Bourouis, S. Alalmaei, W. Shao, C. Yuan · Journal of King Saud University – Computer and Information Sciences · First author

RL–LLMEdge roboticsNavigation

The research question

How can a low-power robot escape a non-convex obstacle trap without constantly running an expensive language model?

A stateless PPO policy handles frequent motion decisions while a local language model is called only when a physical-displacement detector finds that the agent is stuck. Spatial observations are serialized as compact ASCII topology.

Reported results

95%Average success rate · Five simulation levels
72%Pure PPO baseline · Same evaluation suite
90%Edge test success · Hardest map · Orange Pi

Numbers refer to the specific evaluation in the linked publication; they are not universal performance claims.

Figure from the paper

Approach

  1. Reactive control
    PPO handles the ordinary navigation loop without the overhead of invoking an LLM at every step.
  2. Detect actual stagnation
    A sliding-window displacement test recognizes deadlock even when a policy remains overconfident.
  3. Reason, verify, recover
    An LLM proposes a topological subgoal, while lower-level checks reject or recover from unsafe suggestions.

Main findings

  • Deadlock recovery matters
    The RL–LLM combination achieves 95% average success, compared with 72% for pure PPO over five levels.
  • Cognitive calls are gated
    The robot invokes high-level reasoning on physically detected failure rather than using it continuously.
  • On-device check
    An Orange Pi test with a 1.2B model reports 90% success on the hardest map and 37.82 seconds per LLM call.

Scope and limitations

The central evaluation isolates a 2-D grid-level cognitive planning problem. The 37.82-second edge inference latency remains a substantial obstacle for time-critical replanning.

Paper abstract

A decoupled robot navigation architecture combining reinforcement-learning control with language-model guidance to handle partially observable operating conditions.
