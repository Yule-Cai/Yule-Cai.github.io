Research Overview · Yule Cai

Trustworthy and Adaptive Robot Autonomy

My work spans motion planning and runtime assurance, reliable robot decision-making, and multi-robot reinforcement learning. These are complementary research areas—not successive stages of one completed navigation system. Distributed RL communication is a related systems direction.

Explore selected work ↗Discuss research

01 · Motion Planning & Constraint Enforcement

Published: KA-IHO studies collision-free mobile-robot path planning with kinematic feasibility and trajectory refinement. Read the KA-IHO paper summary ↗

In experiment: RuleSync investigates snapshot-consistent motion authorization under changing spatial rules, including admission, revocation and renewed authorization. Mechanism tests and developmental evaluations should not be described as broadly validated field deployments.

02 · Reliable Robot Decision-Making

Published: A decoupled RL–LLM navigation architecture combines low-level reinforcement-learning control with event-triggered high-level guidance under partial observability. Read the published navigation study ↗

Further investigations: Graph-grounded route auditing, instruction-faithful task recovery and language-grounded safety specifications ask whether seemingly plausible actions remain faithful to task intent and execution constraints. Work under review is not presented here as accepted research.

03 · Multi-Robot Learning & Coordination

Ongoing work examines how high-level coordination and low-level policies interact, how execution feedback can guide adaptation, and when teammates should wait, yield or revise their shared task. LLM guidance is studied as a method or comparison—not assumed to be the only coordinator.

Current prototypes and paired trajectories provide development evidence. Generalization across maps, teammates and capabilities remains an open research problem rather than a published performance claim.

Supporting Systems Research · Distributed Reinforcement Learning

CAMS explores opportunity-capped multipath communication scheduling and policy-version-age tail behavior in distributed RL. Its communication and systems objectives should not be equated with robot navigation success rates.

Published Evidence & Benchmark Scope

RL–LLM navigation: 95% mean success vs. 72% for pure PPO

The published evaluation covers five map-difficulty levels and 1,750 trials across the proposed method and seven baselines. These numbers apply to the specified benchmark and do not establish a general real-world success rate.

Long-Term Research Question · Adaptive Joint Capability Models

Proposed research agenda — not a validated method

When a robot team fails, can it distinguish a flawed plan, changes in individual robot capability, and breakdowns in coordination? Can the team update only the affected portion of its shared capability model, and transfer what it learns to new partners?
